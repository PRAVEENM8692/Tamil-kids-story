## 01 hook
Kerala, near Kozhikode. A quiet village.
In one family there, six people die over fourteen years.
Every death looked different. No one suspected anything.
But one thing was common to all of them.
And the one who found it was not a policeman.
It was a younger brother living abroad. This is the Koodathayi case.
Stay till the end, there is a twist.
## 02 disclaimer
One important thing. This case is still being tried in court.
Everyone in it is only an accused. Nothing is proven.
I am telling you the police version and news reports.
All pictures are illustrated depictions.
## 03 intro
The Ponnamattam family of Koodathayi. The head of the house was Annamma Thomas.
She held the family's money and property.
Her daughter-in-law was Jolly. Well dressed, she left every morning with a bag for work.
Where did she work? She told everyone she was a lecturer at NIT Kozhikode.
Remember this. This is the big lie of the story.
## 04 deaths
In two thousand and two, Annamma suddenly collapses and dies.
Everyone thought it was natural. Six years later, her husband Tom Thomas dies.
Three years after that, their son Roy Thomas.
Jolly's husband. The police version is this.
On the day Roy died, he had his favourite chickpea curry and rice.
Did you notice the gaps? Six years, then three, then two and a half.
The investigating officer said so himself. The gap between deaths kept shrinking.
## 05 clue
Here a big mistake happens. When Roy died, a post-mortem was done.
It found cyanide in his body. Cyanide.
A deadly poison. But the local police wrote it off as suicide and closed the case.
No one asked where the poison came from.
That one line in the report slept in a file for eight years.
Roy's uncle Mathew insisted it was not suicide and demanded a proper post-mortem.
In two thousand and fourteen, he too dies.
That same year, a two-year-old child, Alphine, a relative Shaju's daughter.
In two thousand and sixteen, Shaju's wife Sily. Both die.
The very next year, Jolly marries that same Shaju.
## 06 question
Now the question. A case closed for eight years, how did it reopen?
Did a witness speak, did a clue turn up, or did the culprit slip?
Before that, I will tell you, the police did not find this.
A younger brother made them find it.

## 07 answer
Roy's younger brother Rojo lived in America.
When he came home, it bothered him: so many deaths, and his brother's called suicide?
He filed a Right to Information request and got his brother's post-mortem report.
It said cyanide. At the same time, Jolly tried to transfer father Tom's property to her own name.
The proof was a will. The police say Jolly forged it.
With these two, Rojo filed a second complaint.
This time it reached the Kozhikode Rural SP,
K. G. Simon. One thing to notice.
During the investigation, Jolly reportedly contacted Rojo and said they could settle the property dispute, but he must withdraw the complaint.
If there was no crime, why withdraw a complaint?
## 08 arrest
A special team of thirty-five was formed.
They first tested Jolly's lie. They went to NIT and enquired.
No one named Jolly worked there. She was only a graduate, never a lecturer.
Where she went daily, police say, was a tailoring shop, a beauty parlour and two churches.
Another doubt. Police checked whether cyanide was taken from the college lab.
Not a gram was missing. So where did the cyanide come from?
That search showed two people.
A jewellery shop employee Mathew, and Praji Kumar from whom he got it.
On October fourth they opened the graves of six people and took out the remains.
The next day Jolly was arrested. The police say she confessed during interrogation.
She reportedly always carried cyanide in a small box.
She believed that touching it and washing her hands was safe.
She had learned a lot about cyanide, SP
Simon himself has said.
Police say she told them news reports of cyanide deaths inspired her.
The interesting part: it was sodium cyanide.
The police say it cost fifteen hundred rupees or a little more.
A poison that can finish a family, for the price of a meal.
## 09 court
Where is the case now? Six chargesheets have been filed.
A separate case for each death. According to news I saw, the trial is still on.
There is no verdict so far.
In the trial, a close friend of Jolly reportedly testified that she admitted it to him.
The court must still decide this.
News also said Jolly refused a polygraph test citing her health.
Jolly's side denies the allegations.
Until the verdict, she is only an accused.
## 10 ending
This story tells us one thing. One line in a file, nobody read for eight years.
A brother read it, and the truth of six lives came out.
A documentary on this case also came out in two thousand and twenty-three.
