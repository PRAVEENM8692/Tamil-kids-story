## 02 disclaimer
This is a case that is still unsolved and under investigation.
No one has been declared guilty. No one has been arrested so far.
What we tell is only what has appeared in news reports.
Some details are unconfirmed, and we tell them as they are.
All pictures are illustrations.
## 03 intro
In the last part we saw this. In the early hours of October 12, Hemalatha vanished from Alampoondi village.
The local police searched for ten days without result.
On October 22, the DGP transferred the case to the CB-CID.
Political parties also took up the issue.
The state Law Minister said the police are investigating from every angle.
In this part, let us see what the CB-CID has done.
## 04 investigation
The CB-CID set up a temporary investigation centre in the village itself.
That is their office. Their first job was to take the attendance registers of the school the girl attended.
They questioned the teachers. Next, they searched the areas around the house again.
Together with the Forest Department, they decided to search the Alampoondi forest area too.
Another matter. A piece of information came that the girl was in Bengaluru.
So the father's relatives were questioned.
Nothing came of it.
## 05 clue
But since searching in the village brought no result, the CB-CID turned to digital clues.
Here is an interesting fact. Mobile towers keep a record of all the phones working in the area at a given time.
This is called a tower dump. The police get the list of all the numbers that were there at that time.
The CB-CID obtained the data of three towers that were working around Alampoondi at midnight on the day of the incident.
It had more than six hundred mobile numbers.
They are examining those numbers one by one and monitoring the suspicious ones.
They also looked at CCTV footage from Alampoondi, Thiruvalai and Nadupattu.
They are also looking at the angle that a group moving about may have abducted her.

## 07 answer
According to the reports, the CB-CID DIG came to the village and questioned the grandmother, mother and father separately, with the house door locked.
The police also searched the house. Reports say they took the parents' mobile phones and bank passbooks.
The family is connected with brick-kiln work.
Forensic experts went to that kiln again and took away ash and soil samples.
Here we must be clear. This is only one part of the investigation.
In a missing-child case, questioning the family is routine procedure.
It does not mean they committed any crime.
One news site, pointing to investigation sources, says the parents' statements contradict one another.
But this is not an official finding. No charge has been recorded against anyone.
## 08 arrest
So far there has been no arrest in this case. On the thirty-third day, November 14.
DMK deputy general secretary and MP Kanimozhi met the girl's parents and consoled them.
She accused the government of not taking enough action.
She demanded that the Chief Minister intervene directly.
The government has said it is investigating from every angle.
Some reports are still to come. The forensic report, bank and phone data.
One report expects that once these arrive, major action may follow.
But that is only an expectation.
## 09 court
Let us see what has been found so far and what has not.
Found: six hundred phone numbers, three towers' data, some CCTV footage, forensic samples.
Not found: the girl, a solid witness, a CCTV frame, a phone call demanding money.
No arrest, no chargesheet, nothing. In one video on the internet, they say a skeleton was found in the search.
But we have not seen this confirmed in reliable news reports.
So do not take this as true.
Do not spread rumours.
