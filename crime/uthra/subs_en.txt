## 07 answer
What the police did was extraordinary. They ran a test with a live cobra.
Under DySP Asokan, the team laid a dummy the size of Uthra on a cot.
They tied a piece of chicken to its arm.
They released the cobra. It touched the dummy many times but did not bite.
After several provocations, it bit just once.
In that natural bite, the distance between the two fangs was one and three-quarter centimetres.
Next, they held the snake's head and pressed it onto the chicken to make it bite.
In those bites the distance was two to two and a half centimetres.
On Uthra's body it was up to two point eight centimetres.
That proved someone had opened the snake's jaw and made it bite.
There is more. This cobra can only raise a third of its body length.
So it could not have climbed the window ledge into the room.
All three air vents of the room were sealed.
They dug up the buried cobra.
Its stomach was empty. A snake takes about seven days to digest a meal.
That means it had been kept confined for at least seven days.
Experts also spoke about the Russell's viper from March.
It does not live in wet soil, and cannot climb up to an upper floor on a smooth floor.
Locals said no viper had been seen in that area for fifteen years.
## 08 arrest
24 May 2020. Under Crime Branch DySP Asokan, Sooraj, snake catcher Suresh and another associate were arrested.
Suresh admitted selling both snakes to Sooraj.
But he said he had no part in the murder plan.
Suresh said Sooraj called him, told him not to tell anyone, and said they could call it a snake curse.
The Kollam Chief Judicial Magistrate granted Suresh a pardon.
He became a prosecution witness. Traces of sleeping pills were found in Uthra's urine.
Snake searches on Sooraj's mobile, call records, sleeping pills in his car, the plastic jar that held the cobra, all of this the police presented as evidence.
In August 2020, Sooraj's mother, sister and father were also arrested.
The charges were conspiracy, domestic violence and destroying evidence.
14 August 2020. A chargesheet of nearly one thousand pages was filed.
The Forest Department also filed a separate case against Sooraj and Suresh under the Wildlife Protection Act.
## 09 court
The trial was held in 2021. Vava Suresh came as a witness.
He said that in thirty years Russell's vipers had bitten him sixteen times and cobras three hundred and forty times.
He said a snake that bites to protect itself does not bite twice.
It would have woken her from sleep, had she not been sedated.
11 October 2021. The Kollam Additional Sessions Court declared Sooraj guilty.
The charges were murder, attempt to murder, administering poison and destroying evidence.
13 October. Judge M. Manoj announced the sentence.
Double life imprisonment. Before that, separate terms of ten years and seven years.
A fine of five lakh rupees. The judge said the crime was cruel and shocking.
But he said it did not fall under the rarest of rare category for the death penalty.
Uthra's mother Manimekhala had hoped for the death penalty.
So she was disappointed. Sooraj's lawyer called it a moral punishment and said they would appeal.
