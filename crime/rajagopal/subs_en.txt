## 01 hook
31 October 2001. A forest in the Kodaikanal hills.
Forest department staff spot a body in the woods.
Strangled. Unidentified.
But eighteen years later, because of this body, the Supreme Court upheld a life sentence for a hotelier whose chain had over eighty branches across the world.
The man behind the idli and dosa that lakhs of people eat every day.
Known as the Dosa King. One word from an astrologer.
Believing it, he set out to marry a schoolgirl.
That desire took a life. What happened?

## 02 disclaimer
This is a real case that the courts have fully decided.
I am telling it only on the basis of the High Court and Supreme Court judgments and news reports.
All pictures are illustrations. There are no photographs of real people.
## 03 intro
First, let's see who he is. Pitchai Rajagopal.
A tiny village called Punnaiyadi in Thoothukudi district.
His father sold onions. The house had a mud floor.
Rajagopal left school after Class 7 and took a job wiping tables at a restaurant.
They say he slept on the kitchen floor itself.
Then a job in a grocery shop. In 1968, in K. K.
Nagar, then Chennai's outskirts, he started his own small grocery shop.
1979. A small incident. A salesman who came to the shop said:
there isn't a single restaurant here. To eat, you have to go all the way to T. Nagar.
That one remark stayed in Rajagopal's mind.
He decided to open a restaurant. To be exact, 14 December 1981.
He took over a failing restaurant called Kamatchi Bhavan, renamed it Saravana Bhavan and opened it.
He began it while deep in debt.
Here is an interesting point. Reports say astrology played a big role in his growth.
One report says an astrologer told him to work in a fire-related business, and that is why he entered the hotel trade.
The sambar and chutney taste the same in every branch.
Customers say that is his strength.
In 2000, the first branch abroad, in Dubai. Then branches spread across the world.
But behind this success, there was a dark side too.
## 04 investigation
1994. A get-together for Saravana Bhavan employees.
There, Rajagopal sees a schoolgirl.
Jeevajyothi, a Class 12 student, daughter of an employee at the Chennai branch.
Rajagopal was already married twice.
Once in 1972. Once in 1994.
But according to reports, his astrologer told him that marrying this girl would make his business grow even more.
The High Court judgment also mentions this.
But Jeevajyothi had someone else in her heart.
The tutor who came to teach her younger brother maths, Prince Santhakumar.
Santhakumar was of a different religion, so Jeevajyothi's father did not accept it.
In April 1999 the two eloped and registered their marriage.
Rajagopal did not stop there. He sent them gifts of jewellery, clothes and money.
Reports say he gave them one lakh rupees to start a business.
Jeevajyothi accepted the gifts. But she did not accept him.
For Rajagopal this was an insult. Anger: who dares refuse me?
## 05 clue
28 September 2001. Rajagopal goes to the couple's house in the middle of the night.
He threatens Santhakumar: end this relationship within two days.
The two tried to escape.
But five employees led by Saravana Bhavan manager Daniel cornered them, pushed them into a car and took them to a godown in K. K.
Nagar.
There Rajagopal beat Santhakumar badly.
Jeevajyothi cried and begged them to let him go.
12 October. The two played a trick.
They said they were going out to a felicitation function for Rajagopal, and went straight to the Chennai police commissioner's office and filed a complaint.
But it did not end there. A few days later, Rajagopal's men picked them up again.
This time, reports say, Rajagopal held a copy of the complaint given at the commissioner's office.
Then they took Santhakumar away separately.
## 06 question
Now a key twist in the story. Rajagopal paid Daniel five lakh rupees to kill Santhakumar.
But Daniel changed his mind.
Without killing him, he gave him five thousand rupees and told him to run to Mumbai and never come back.
Santhakumar spoke to Jeevajyothi on the phone. He said Rajagopal paid five lakh to have him killed.
But Daniel let him go. If you were Santhakumar, what would you do?
Run to Mumbai, save your life and leave your wife, or come back?

## 07 answer
Santhakumar came back. He could not bring himself to run and leave his wife.
Jeevajyothi, her father, mother and brother all went to Rajagopal together.
To plead with him to leave them alone. Rajagopal told them to wait in the next room and called Daniel.
He asked what had happened to Santhakumar. Daniel did not know they had come.
So he told a lie. We tied Santhakumar and left him on the railway track.
The train ran over him. We burned his clothes.
Then Rajagopal called Santhakumar in.
According to one report, he mocked Daniel: what is this, Santhakumar's ghost?
Daniel, furious that his betrayal was exposed, beat Santhakumar.
After that, Jeevajyothi's family was put in a van and taken to a faraway village.
There, they are said to have performed rituals, saying she was possessed and it had to be driven out.
Two days later, Daniel and a few others put Santhakumar in a car.
Hours of travel north. 26 October. Near Kodaikanal.
They strangled Santhakumar to death and threw his body into the forest.
## 08 arrest
31 October. In the Kodaikanal hills, forest staff in the forest area found a body.
The post-mortem concluded death by asphyxia from strangulation.
By now Jeevajyothi was suspicious. Because a priest came to the house and said a ritual for a woman who had lost her husband had to be done.
She had thought Santhakumar was still alive.
This ritual was a huge shock to Jeevajyothi.
As soon as she was allowed to go home, she filed complaints with the police commissioner and at the Velachery police station.
Jeevajyothi identified the body found at Kodaikanal.
It was her husband, Santhakumar. When news of the murder spread, Daniel and his men surrendered in court.
Rajagopal surrendered after them.
2004. The sessions court gave Rajagopal and the others ten years in prison.
It did not call it murder, but culpable homicide not amounting to murder, that is, under IPC Section 304.
Part One.
It also imposed a fine of several lakh rupees.
But after eight months in prison, the Supreme Court suspended the sentence on health grounds.
The prosecution appealed to increase the sentence.
## 09 court
19 March 2009. The Madras High Court. Justices R.
Banumathi and P. K. Misra.
Calling it a major planned murder, it set aside the ten-year sentence and gave Rajagopal and the others life imprisonment for murder.
In the judgment, the court noted that the prosecution had proved Rajagopal wanted to marry Jeevajyothi believing, on an astrologer's word, that his business would grow.
It also reduced the fine. Even then, they went to the Supreme Court.
The Supreme Court, on health grounds, granted them bail back in 2009.
So they stayed outside for ten years.
In total, one report says Rajagopal spent only eleven months in jail.
29 March 2019. Eighteen years after the crime, a bench of Justices N. V.
Ramana, Mohan M. Shantanagoudar and Indira Banerjee delivered the verdict.
Life sentence upheld.
The court said the involvement of all the appellants in strangling Santhakumar and throwing the body was proved.
The prosecution relied on three things.
Motive, who was last seen with Santhakumar, and the body recovered at the spot Daniel showed.
Rajagopal's side made one argument.
That when a first information report already existed, registering a second one was illegal.
The Supreme Court rejected it.
## 10 ending
The Supreme Court gave Rajagopal time until 7 July 2019 to surrender.
He was seventy-one and unwell. On 7 July he did not surrender.
Citing health, he asked for more time.
The Supreme Court refused. It told him to surrender immediately.
9 July. He surrendered. He was admitted to Stanley Hospital.
13 July. Heart attack. Ventilator support. 18 July 2019.
Rajagopal died. Heart attack. He was seventy-one.
Let us note a few things from this case.
One. Jeevajyothi. An ordinary family's daughter.
She fought for eighteen years and won justice.
There is also news that a film is being made about it.
Two. It makes us think about when belief crosses the line.
Believing in astrology is a personal choice. But when that belief goes so far as to take another person's life, it is a crime.
Three. Even in a time without CCTV and mobile phones, this case broke because of one woman's courage and one complaint.
