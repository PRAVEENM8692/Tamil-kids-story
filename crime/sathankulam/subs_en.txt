## 01 hook
2020, June 19. The COVID lockdown. Sathankulam, Thoothukudi district.
At 7:30 in the evening, a mobile-shop owner is taken to the police station.
The allegation: he kept his shop open past the permitted time.
Within three days, both father and son were dead.
But the story of this case does not end there, because the men accused were sworn to uphold the law.
They tried to cover it up. Let's see how that cover-up was broken.
## 02 disclaimer
One important thing. The trial court has delivered its verdict in this case.
But the death sentences must be confirmed by the High Court.
An appeal may also follow. What I say is based only on the court verdict, the CBI chargesheet and news reports.
All the pictures are illustrated depictions.
## 03 intro
Jayaraj, 59 years old, ran a small shop in Sathankulam.
His son Bennix was 31.
He also had a mobile shop nearby.
Neither of them had any criminal case against them until then.
It was lockdown time, with strict shop timings.
When police were on patrol, they said the shop was open, and an argument broke out between Jayaraj and the police.
Nobody imagined that argument would end with two lives lost.
## 04 disappearance
June 19, 7:30 in the evening. Jayaraj is taken to Sathankulam police station.
When Bennix learns his father has been arrested, he goes to the station with a friend.
He was waiting outside when they called him in.
Then they kept him there too.
The lawyers and friends standing outside were not allowed in.
According to the CBI chargesheet, the station door was locked.
Through the night, both men were allegedly beaten in several rounds.
They say it continued until 3 in the morning.
The CBI says the violence grew worse after Sub-Inspector Raghu Ganesh arrived at 11:30 at night.
It is also alleged that he told them to start beating again whenever things went quiet.
The next morning, both men were taken to Kovilpatti Government Hospital.
They obtained a medical certificate there.
Then they were produced before a magistrate and sent to Kovilpatti sub-jail, 100 kilometres away.
There is one more shocking detail.
It is said that both men bled so much that their lungis had to be changed six times.
Even so, a fitness certificate was issued at the Kovilpatti hospital.
A complaint was later raised that the Sathankulam magistrate, citing COVID protocols, remanded them without seeing their injuries or speaking to them.
On June 22, at 9 at night, Bennix died.
The next day, at 4:30 in the morning, Jayaraj died too.
## 05 clue
Nobody outside could have known what had happened to the two men.
But the shopkeepers of Sathankulam came out onto the streets.
Road blockades. On social media, a hashtag demanding justice for Jayaraj and Bennix started trending.
That noise was heard across India. The then Chief Minister's remark that they died of illness became a major controversy.
At the same time, the Madurai Bench of the High Court took up the case on its own.
It ordered that the post-mortem be video-recorded and conducted by three doctors.
And then another thing. For the inquiry, the Thoothukudi district collector himself took control of Sathankulam police station.
They say this was the first time in the history of Indian policing.
The reason: so the police could not destroy the records.
On June 24, three officers including Inspector Sridhar were suspended.
All the police in that station were transferred.
On the 26th, the hashtag seeking justice trended among the top thirty worldwide.
Cricketers, actors and many others spoke out.
The government announced ten lakh rupees and a government job for each family.
After a complaint that a constable misbehaved with the magistrate who came to inquire, the High Court itself began contempt proceedings.
## 06 question
Now the question. When the police themselves try to hide what happened inside a police station, what brings the truth out?
A CCTV camera, a witness, or the blood on the wall?

## 07 answer
The answer: all three. They supported each other. First, the walls and the floor.
The CBI collected blood samples from the station.
The Delhi forensic laboratory said the blood on the walls, the inspector's room floor and the bathroom floor matched the DNA of Jayaraj and Bennix.
That report came in October 2020. The DNA on the wooden sticks seized from the police also matched the same two men.
Second, the CCTV.
The case says the cameras inside the station were set to erase their recordings automatically every day.
But cameras from the shops around helped establish that the police arrived before the curfew began.
In other words, it supported the timeline shown by the prosecution.
Third, a woman police officer. She was in the station that night.
She testified in court. She said the father and son were beaten all night.
News reports say her account matched the CCTV evidence.
The testimony of someone from inside became one of the pillars of the case.
## 08 arrest
First, the Tamil Nadu CB-CID began the investigation.
The murder charge was added. On July 7, the case went to the CBI.
They registered two cases.
The very next day, the Friends of Police organisation across Tamil Nadu was disbanded.
The CBI arrested ten people: Inspector Sridhar, Sub-Inspectors Balakrishnan and Raghu Ganesh, head constables and constables.
One of those ten, Sub-Inspector Pauldurai, died of COVID during the investigation.
In September 2020, the CBI filed a chargesheet of over two thousand pages.
In August 2022, a supplementary chargesheet.
It also included video analysis. What did the accused say?
That the cause was a lockdown violation and a verbal quarrel, and that there was no conspiracy.
## 09 court
The trial began in Madurai in February 2021.
It ran for more than five years, with over fifty witnesses.
On March 23, 2026, the Madurai First Additional Sessions Court held all nine policemen guilty.
For murder and for destroying evidence.
On April 6 came the sentence. Judge G. Muthukumaran sentenced all nine to death.
He called it a rarest of rare case.
One thing he said is important. If ordinary people had committed this crime, there would be one punishment.
But these were protectors of the law, mentally sound, educated, drawing government salaries.
In the judgment he mentioned the George Floyd killing in America and the custodial death of Ajith Kumar in Tamil Nadu.
The court said their motive was vengeance and spreading fear in others.
Of the nine convicted, there is one inspector, two sub-inspectors, two head constables and four constables.
A compensation of one crore forty lakh rupees was also ordered.
The death sentences must be confirmed by the Madras High Court.
