## 01 hook
A young woman had been missing for two and a half months.
Calls to her number went unanswered.
Yet friends who messaged her got replies from her account.
"I'm fine, I'm in Rishikesh."
She never sent that reply. The police say it was the man living with her.
What broke the lie was not a witness. It was a bank transaction and a phone location.
This is the Shraddha Walkar case. Stay till the end, and I'll tell you where he slipped.
## 02 disclaimer
One important note. This case is still being heard in the Saket court in Delhi.
Aaftab Poonawala is only an accused.
He has denied the charge and said he will face the trial.
What I tell you comes from the police chargesheet and news reports.
All pictures are drawn depictions.
## 03 intro
Shraddha grew up in Vasai, near Mumbai.
She worked at a call centre, where she met Aaftab through the Bumble app.
In 2019 she began living with him, against her family's objections.
But it was not a peaceful life. In November 2020 Shraddha herself filed a police complaint.
She wrote that he had beaten her and nearly suffocated her.
The police chargesheet itself says this.
Later the two left Mumbai for Delhi and, on the fifteenth of May, moved into a rented flat near Chhatarpur.
She was alive in that house for only three more days.
## 04 cover
The eighteenth of May. According to the police, the two had a big fight that afternoon.
A neighbour says he heard the quarrel.
Aaftab is accused of strangling her.
What happened next shocked even the police.
After the killing he did not panic and run. He planned.
The police say that the very next day he bought knives, garbage bags, bleach and a new refrigerator.
Six twenty-litre water cans to wash the floor.
Two days later, eleven kilos of dry ice.
They say he cut the body into thirty-five pieces, stored it in a three-hundred-litre fridge, and over eighteen nights at two in the morning dumped it in the forest.
Reports say he claimed the idea came from the American series Dexter.
## 05 clue
Now the main point. Nobody knew Shraddha was missing.
She used to talk to her friends regularly. Suddenly everything stopped.
Friends told her brother.
Her father filed a complaint with the Vasai police.
Six months on, on the eighth of November, the case reached the Mehrauli police in Delhi.
The police questioned Aaftab.
He said, "On the twenty-second of May she fought with me, took only her phone, and left."
The police began digging into her phone, her bank and her Instagram.
That is where his lie cracked. Even after she supposedly left, her phone's location stayed with his phone.
The GPS of her Bumble account showed the same thing.
At the same time, fifty-four thousand rupees went from her bank account to Aaftab's account.
## 06 question
Imagine you are the police now.
He says she left the house.
Money has come from her account. Replies are coming to messages.
Which one of these completely breaks his lie? Pause and think.
## 07 answer
The answer is: the place.
If she had really left, her phone, her payment and her Instagram message should have come from somewhere else.
But all three were in the Mehrauli area.
That is, exactly where he was.
Even her Instagram chat with a friend on the thirty-first of May came from that same area.
Another interesting detail.
Six days after the killing, on the twenty-fourth of May, he met another Bumble woman and gave her a ring as a gift.
The police say it was Shraddha's ring.
That ring was later recovered from the woman.
One more thing. On the night of the killing, only a single chicken roll was ordered on Zomato.
At noon, food for two. At night, for one.
The police read that to mean she was no longer alive.
## 08 arrest
The police questioned him again and again.
There were holes in what he said. First he claimed he burned the bones and ground them.
Then he said he cut her into seventeen pieces.
Arrested on the twelfth of November. The police say he admitted the crime.
He took the police to the Chhatarpur forest.
With the help of a sniffer dog, they recovered thirteen body parts.
Mostly bones. The head was not found immediately.
When tested against her father's DNA, it was confirmed to be Shraddha's.
## 09 court
In just seventy-five days the police filed a chargesheet of over six thousand six hundred pages.
More than a hundred witnesses. On the ninth of May 2023 the court framed charges of murder and destruction of evidence.
Aaftab said, "I do not plead guilty.
I will face the trial." There is no verdict to this day.
Since July 2026, hearings have been held every day.
A DNA expert has finished giving evidence.
Her father, who fought for Shraddha, died in 2025,
without seeing the verdict.
## 10 ending
This case tells us one thing.
If someone has no contact at all for two and a half months, when should we start to suspect?
Is it enough that her phone gets replies? How do we know it is really her?
