## 03 intro
His name was Gowri Shankar. Born in a village near Vellore.
He came to Chennai young and lived in a slum in Mylapore.
First a cycle rickshaw, then an auto. That is why the name, "Auto Shankar".
From Thiruvanmiyur to Mahabalipuram, there were small fishing hamlets along the coast.
They brewed illicit liquor there. Shankar loaded it into his auto and sold it in Chennai.
That liquor trade introduced him to another business.
An illegal trade involving women in Mahabalipuram.
It is said his auto also carried women between that town and Chennai.
He learned well how that trade worked.
Then he started his own. A lodge on LB Road.
Huts in Periyar Nagar, Thiruvanmiyur.
That became his base. This trade was risky.
So, for protection, he did something.
It is said he made police officers and politicians his customers.
## 04 investigation
An ordinary auto driver had now become the man who ruled the area.
He built a house in Periyar Nagar and lived with his wife and children.
A journalist says many police officers and influential people came to that house-warming.
Shankar is said to have recorded the event on video.
Why video? It is said he held video tapes and pictures of his customers.
That journalist suspects it was for blackmail.
And that is not all.
He reportedly had pictures of women who worked for him and of his lovers. It is said police seized them and they may have been destroyed later.
He had a regular haunt: a cabaret called "Pals" on Mount Road near Devi theatre.
He went there often. That is where he saw Lalitha.
A dancer there. Shankar became infatuated with her.
One report says she was his third wife.
Lalitha's story is the mysterious start of this case.
## 05 clue
Lalitha fell in love with Shankar's friend Sudalaimuthu.
The two ran away. Shankar found them and brought them back to his den.
October 1987, they say. Lalitha was killed and buried in a plot in Periyar Nagar.
And what did he do with that spot? He built a house there and rented it to an old widow for 150 rupees a month.
That woman lived there without knowing a body lay beneath her.
Sudalaimuthu's story is even more horrifying. His body was burned and the ashes scattered in the Bay of Bengal, they say.
When burn marks showed in the house, he reportedly explained, "We were roasting meat."
To those asking about Lalitha, he reportedly said, "She has gone on a tour of India."
Sudalaimuthu's friend Ravi asked questions.
He too was killed and buried outside the house.
It was reportedly passed off as a place where liquor was hidden.
He even sent Ravi's wife a false letter saying Ravi was in Mumbai.
There is another murder story.
One report says three men tried to take a woman from the LB Road lodge, a quarrel followed, and Shankar's gang killed them and sealed them inside a wall.
Another account says the three belonged to a rival gang.
It says they were killed for demanding protection money.
The two accounts conflict.
Either way, on 29 May 1988, Sampath, Mohan and Govindaraj are alleged to have been killed and buried.
## 06 question
Now comes the terrifying part of the story. In 1988, nine young women vanished from their homes.
Nobody knows why or how.
Reports say these cases made the police suspect a serial killer.
What happened to those women?
One account says they too were burned like Sudalaimuthu and the ashes dissolved in the sea.
Another big claim. Shortly before his execution, Shankar is said to have made a statement.
Reports say he said the women were abducted to meet the demands of powerful politicians.
But this has not been fully proven. What happened to the women is still not fully known.
Meanwhile, Sampath's wife Vijaya files a complaint at Mylapore police station.
"My husband is missing. I suspect Auto Shankar."
The police told her to go to Thiruvanmiyur.
There, police arrested Shankar on a minor "nuisance" charge and let him go.
Vijaya was sent away: "Don't trouble us again."

## 07 answer
But Vijaya did not give up. She wrote a petition to the Governor.
Even though the Governor asked for an inquiry, the police stayed sluggish.
Next she went to a neighbour who was a journalist.
His article raised the suspicion that the three men may have been murdered.
July 1988. Newspapers brought these crimes into the open for the first time.
Chennai was shaken. By then the political climate had also changed.
Reports say the change in political climate was a major reason for Shankar's downfall.
A senior police officer ordered a special inquiry.
It brought out the deaths of Sampath, Mohan and Govindaraj.
Their bodies were reportedly taken out of Shankar's place in Thiruvanmiyur.
Lalitha's body was also dug up in Periyar Nagar.
Journalists too went there and asked questions.
What they found was a shock.
A widow was living in the house there, paying 150 rupees rent.
When police arrested and questioned Shankar, he is said to have confessed to the three 1988 murders and three more.
Six in all.
## 08 arrest
Shankar's brother Mohan, Eldin, Shivaji and other associates were also added to the case.
Then came another big twist.
20 August 1990. Shankar escaped from a Chennai prison.
Along with his brother Mohan and some associates.
The court later said some prison guards helped.
They were charged with criminal conspiracy and given six months' rigorous imprisonment each.
Shankar was caught again. Mohan stayed free for years and was reportedly caught in Pune on 25 June 1992.
## 09 court
The case was tried at the Chengalpattu sessions court.
31 May 1991. Shankar, Eldin and Shivaji were sentenced to death.
27 April 1995. Shankar was hanged at Salem Central Prison.
He was forty-one. His brother Mohan was later given three life sentences for six murders.
This case inspired several films.
The 1990 film "Pulan Visaranai" is said to be partly inspired by his crimes.
A web series followed in 2019.
## 10 ending
Another strange thing. The people of Periyar Nagar still remember him as a generous man who helped others.
But to the city, he was the killer who shook Chennai.
His family still lives in Periyar Nagar.
The final questions in this case remain unanswered.
What happened to those nine women? Where did the video tapes go?
Who were his customers? None of this has fully come out.
