## 01 hook
Karnataka, 2009. Several young women are found dead in bus-stand toilets.
Each one in a different town, on a different day.
The police write every case off as suicide and close the files.
But one woman's mobile number is about to unravel this whole mystery.
How did a single mobile phone give away a serial killer?

## 02 disclaimer
This is a real case in which the court has delivered its verdict.
We tell it based on press reports.
Some details differ between reports, and we say so where they appear.
All pictures are illustrations.
## 03 intro
His name was Mohan Kumar. Born in 1963.
He worked for twenty-three years as a physical education teacher at a primary school in the Dakshina Kannada region.
He left that job in 2003. A school teacher is respected in his town.
Nobody would suspect him. He turned that very respect into his weapon.
His targets were poor women who were looking for a groom to marry.
Some reports say he won the trust of poor, Dalit and tribal women by promising to marry them without dowry.
A different name every time. In one case, a court report says he introduced himself as Shashidhar Pujari.
Another report says he told a different woman he was a plantation supervisor.
His method was very simple. A meeting at a bus stand.
A love act. A promise of marriage.
What happened between that night and the next morning is the terrifying part.
## 04 investigation
He would take the woman to a lodge near the bus stand.
They would stay there that night. Before leaving in the morning, he would say one thing.
"You must not get pregnant before the wedding.
Take this pill." The woman believed it was a contraceptive pill.
In truth it was cyanide. And there was another trick.
He would tell her to go and take the pill in the bus-stand toilet.
Police say he convinced her with the excuse that she might vomit or have trouble.
The woman would go in and swallow the pill.
Within seconds she would collapse.
Meanwhile, Mohan would go back to the lodge, take the jewellery she had left there, and leave, the court was told.
He would sell that jewellery in Mangaluru. So why did the police find nothing?
The women were found dead in different towns.
Mysuru, Hassan, Bengaluru. Each town's police recorded it as suicide, or as the death of an unclaimed person.
One more thing. Many families did not even file a missing-person complaint, even when the daughter did not return.
Even the complaints some did file were not taken seriously by the police.
So not one or two, but many lives were lost.
Nobody realised this was the work of one man.
## 05 clue
June 17, 2009. Parimaru village, Bantwal taluk.
Anitha, aged twenty-two, left her home.
She never returned. Anitha's parents filed a complaint.
At first the police did not take it seriously.
But then another problem rose in the town.
Some organisations alleged that Anitha had been abducted by a man of another religion and began protests.
The crowd surrounded the Bantwal police station itself.
Reports say they threatened to burn down the station.
Under that pressure, the police formed a large investigation team.
They asked for one month. That pressure turned out to be a blessing.
Otherwise this case would have been closed as just "a girl ran away".
The police pulled Anitha's mobile call records and her home landline calls.
In that list, one number looked different.
That number was a SIM card in the name of Sridhar of Madikeri.
The police went looking for Sridhar. He gave them a piece of information.
His sister Kaveri had gone missing a few months earlier.
Her mobile phone had gone missing along with her.
So the number that had been talking to Anitha belonged to another missing woman's phone.
## 06 question
Now the police had a strange puzzle.
A call had been made to Anitha from the phone of another woman who was dead.
Who had been using that phone? The police traced the calls to and from that phone.
Kaveri's phone records led them to two more women.
Vinutha from Puttur, and Pushpa from the Kerala border region.
Both of them had also gone missing.
Now many dots began to show on the police map.
One woman's phone had called another woman.
That other woman had also vanished. Think for yourself.
So many phones, so many women. There must be one person common to all of them.

## 07 answer
The police did one thing. They scientifically analysed all the call records they had.
Then came a surprise. All of those numbers had, at least once, been active in the same area near Mangaluru.
That meant someone had carried these phones to that area.
Several police teams descended on the place.
They traced the owner of one phone found there.
He was a young man, a relative of Mohan. "The person who gave me this phone was my uncle, Mohan Kumar.
He is a teacher," he said. So after Anitha died, Mohan had given her phone to his relative as a gift.
One newspaper says he threw away Kaveri's phone.
Now the police had a name. Mohan Kumar.
What next? They had to catch him. But he was a slippery man.
How did the police catch him?
## 08 arrest
There are two accounts of this.
One report says the police phoned Mohan from Anitha's phone and waited at a bus stand.
They arrested him when he arrived.
Another report says they summoned him to the police station on the pretext of questioning in another case, and arrested him there.
Either way, on October 21, 2009, the Bantwal police arrested Mohan Kumar.
That was about four months after Anitha went missing.
From him the police seized eight cyanide tablets, four mobile phones and Anitha's jewellery.
Where did the cyanide come from? The police IG said it was bought from a local goldsmith.
A case has been filed against the person who sold it as well.
Here is another important detail. Mohan tried to kill one woman, but she escaped.
She did not take the pill he gave her on April 13, 2009.
Reports say she is the only living witness against him.
## 09 court
During interrogation, Mohan confessed to killing twenty women.
The police re-examined the files of missing women and the records of women found dead at bus stands.
That was when the separate cases came together as one.
Twenty cases were filed against Mohan. In 2013, he was given the death sentence in Anitha's murder case.
In 2017, it was reduced to life imprisonment.
In one case after another, he kept being found guilty.
In June 2020, he was declared guilty in the twentieth murder case as well.
Some reports say there may be more victims than this.
But that has not been proved. So the number the court confirmed is twenty.
Many reports say this story inspired the web series "Dahaad".
## 10 ending
Let us note a few things from this case.
One. Nobody saw the connection between so many deaths because each town's police looked at them separately.
One mobile number joined those separate dots into one.
Two. Anitha's case was taken seriously because of a protest.
Without that protest, it might not have come out for many more years.
This is a lesson for every family. Never stop filing a complaint.
Three.
Even when someone looks respectable, like a school teacher, it matters how much you find out about a person before you trust them.
